\section{Data}\label{sec:data}

\paragraph{Olivetti Faces.} Olivetti \citep{samaria1994} provides 400 greyscale images of 40 people, ten each, at $64\times64$ pixels in $[0,1]$. The images are tightly cropped and roughly eye-aligned: across one person's ten photographs the eyes sit in nearly the same place, and what changes is expression, head tilt, and lighting. This is the best case for raw pixels, because the geometric normalisation pixel methods are most sensitive to has already been done; it is also an easy case for a deep model. All Olivetti results should be read as a best-case, optimistic reading for the pixel representation. For the map comparison every image is used once---nothing is trained, so there is no split---and for verification the images are partitioned per identity into five enrolment and five probe images, identically for both maps. All 40 identities are enrolled and there are no unknown-identity probes, so this protocol measures cross-identity confusion rather than open-set rejection of strangers. The dataset-wide pixel range is $[0,1]$, and the preprocessing of the 400-image network batch matches the reference recipe exactly.

\paragraph{Webcam scans.} Twelve scans of the author's face were captured on 6 September 2026 with a laptop webcam, ten consecutive frames each, each frame processed through the same MTCNN and embedding pipeline. The saved artefact per scan is a $(10,512)$ float32 matrix of unit-norm embeddings; raw frames were not retained. Conditions: room light from the front and left, and phone flash from the front, left, right, and below, two repeat scans per condition. Room light was only available from two directions, so the design is unbalanced by necessity (twelve scans, not sixteen). Frames within a scan are consecutive webcam frames of a moving person, not independent photographs: mean embedding distance grows from 0.153 at lag~1 to 0.257 at lag~9, so the effective sample size per scan is below ten and scan means are the more meaningful unit. The first frame of a scan is also systematically different (mean distance 0.243 to the rest of the scan at frame~0 versus 0.167 at frame~5), consistent with webcam warm-up.

\paragraph{Model.} The embedder is Inception-ResNet-v1 pretrained on VGGFace2 \citep{szegedy2017,cao2018} via \texttt{facenet-pytorch} \citep{facenetpytorch}. The checkpoint is \texttt{20180402-114759-\allowbreak vggface2.pt}, loaded in evaluation mode and frozen. It has 23,482,624 embedding parameters; the unused 8,631-class head adds 4,427,703. Outputs are L2-normalised. Nothing is trained or fine-tuned.
