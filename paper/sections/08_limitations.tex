\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{One subject, no stranger.} The webcam study is observational: one subject, conditions fixed by the capture session rather than randomised, and no stranger was ever recorded. Every live distance is therefore same-person and measures convenience, not security. No live threshold can be calibrated from these data, because there is no impostor distribution in the live domain. The cross-domain Olivetti-to-webcam distances are descriptive only; they are not a calibrated impostor distribution and neither an upper nor a lower bound on webcam-domain stranger distance.

\paragraph{Known-population impostors.} The Olivetti verification protocol is closed-set. Its impostors are the other \emph{enrolled} identities from the same 40-person dataset, captured in the same session, so the reported false-accept rates measure cross-identity confusion within a known population. The primary analysis contains no unknown-to-gallery probes and no stranger trials, so its numbers cannot be read as open-set rejection of unknown people or as deployment false-accept rates. An additive identity-disjoint check (13 calibration, 13 evaluation, and 14 unknown-to-gallery identities; five reused partitions; 65 genuine, 1,560 impostor, and 140 unknown probes per partition) selects thresholds on calibration impostor scores only. At a nominal 1\% calibration FAR it reaches held-out TAR 75.08\% (Map A) and 100\% (Map B), but held-out FAR is 1.21\% and 0.77\% respectively, so the 1\% target is not validated out of sample; 12.1\% of unknown-to-gallery identities are still accepted as somebody by Map B (any-of-13-gallery-claims rate; 18.9\% for Map A), while the per-claim held-out FAR is 0.77\%; the two use different denominators. Even the unknown-to-gallery identities remain known to the frozen model and come from the same dataset, and the partitions overlap rather than providing independent samples.

\paragraph{Threshold selection and interpolated EER.} The thresholds at matched FRR and at the EER are selected on the same split scores used to report false-accept and false-reject rates. This is in-sample, descriptive calibration, not a held-out calibration procedure, and it makes the reported operating points optimistic. The EER is interpolated from a finite score grid (FRR resolution 0.5\%, FAR resolution 0.0064\%) and is not necessarily an exactly realizable distance threshold; empirical FAR and FRR at explicit thresholds should be preferred when a specific operating point matters. Map B's 0.22\% EER is below the FRR grid resolution and should be read as ``at the measurement floor'', not as three significant figures.

\paragraph{Small, correlated samples.} There are 400 images, 40 identities, and ten images per identity, all from a single capture session. The 1,800 genuine pairs are not independent (each image appears in nine pairs), and the five 5/5 splits reuse the same images, so split variance is not capture variance. Image-level resampling does not model identity-level or session-level dependence, and no confidence interval here accounts for it; the effective sample size is much smaller than the nominal trial counts.

\paragraph{Olivetti is the best case for raw pixels.} The dataset is tightly cropped and roughly eye-aligned, removing the geometric variability that most damages pixel-space methods. Map A's results are an optimistic reading for raw pixels, and the same property makes the deep model's task easy, so both maps' absolute accuracy is optimistic relative to unconstrained data.

\paragraph{Transductive visualisation and frozen model.} PCA for both 2-D plots is fit on all 400 images, including verification probes. Recognition metrics use the unprojected vectors, so this fit does not show that the measured advantage of Map B is conservative. The embedder was not trained or fine-tuned on either evaluation dataset, but it was pretrained on millions of faces; the observed gap combines architecture, training data and representation and does not isolate any one cause.

\paragraph{The rank control does not explain the gap.} Projecting Map A to its intrinsic 399-dimensional span reproduces its metrics because the projection is distance-preserving, but 399 is not 512 and the two representations have different intrinsic geometry. The control rules out nominal input dimensionality as the source of Map A's metrics; it does not provide a matched-dimensionality explanation of the raw-versus-embedding gap, and no such explanation is claimed.

\paragraph{Webcam frames are not independent.} The ten frames per scan are consecutive webcam frames of a moving person: within-scan distance grows from 0.153 at lag~1 to 0.257 at lag~9, and the first frame is a warm-up outlier. Frame-pair counts overstate the effective sample size, which is why scan means are the more meaningful unit. Raw webcam frames were not retained, so alternate crops, detectors, or preprocessing cannot be re-analysed from the saved data.

\paragraph{Illumination comparison is observational.} The intensity comparison contrasts room light with phone flash, so it changes source spectrum, source size, and shadow structure as well as brightness, not brightness alone. The direction comparison changes the phone position but remains one subject, one session, and two repeats per condition. Room-front and flash-front were captured in separate scans, so no causal attribution to brightness is made. The ``half-shadowed region'' reading is a hypothesis, and the under-lighting observation is descriptive; neither is a controlled illumination experiment.

\paragraph{Detection and thresholds.} The MTCNN implementation crops the O-Net box without a landmark similarity transform, so alignment is detector-box alignment; a landmark-warped pipeline might change the between-condition distances, which this study does not test. Olivetti verification thresholds (EER thresholds 8.51 and 0.84) and the webcam distance scale are not comparable, and no threshold in this paper should be deployed.

\paragraph{No demographic, sensor, or session diversity.} One dataset with limited demographic variety, one laptop webcam, one model and backbone, and no repeated sessions across days. The study cannot speak to fairness, sensor generalisation, or template ageing. The optional synthetic-lighting ablation was not run.
